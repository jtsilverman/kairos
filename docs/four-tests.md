# The four tests

Jake's definition of a working, clear and strong fundamental thesis (2026-09-11, `CLAUDE.md` "The
goal"). Every grade or ruling on a thesis, by the blind sorter or by the funnel's seats, uses these
definitions and no other rubric. This whole file reaches every reader's prompt; how the rubric was
chosen and what it measured live in `docs/four-tests-record.md`, which no reader sees.

## The tests

1. `business_case`: a clear business case for the company's value. What the company sells, to whom,
   why the customer pays, and why that produces value for the shareholder.
2. `fundamentals`: good fundamentals. Revenue and earnings trajectory, margins, cash conversion,
   balance sheet, dilution, and clean forensic ratios.
3. `demand_case`: a case that demand for what the company sells rises over the next quarter to a
   year, and what drives it: a product cycle, a customer's spending shift, a regulation, a price,
   a shortage, a geopolitical shift (reshoring, tariffs, defense, energy policy), or any other named
   cause. Geopolitics is one example of a driver, never the required one.
4. `positioned`: a well functioning company clearly positioned to capture that demand. Capacity,
   share, an execution record, and no visible blocker.

## The grades

- **strong**: the dossier states the evidence and the evidence is decisive.
- **adequate**: the evidence is present with a gap the reader can name.
- **weak**: a claim with thin evidence, or evidence that cuts both ways.
- **absent**: no evidence in the dossier, or the evidence points the other way.

Each grade carries one `evidence` sentence and one `cite`, a doc id that exists in the dossier.

## The driver

One sentence: what moves the price if the thesis is right. The judge's `thesis` paragraph opens with
the driver. The blind sorter records the driver on its own; a grader later asks whether the two name
the same thing.
